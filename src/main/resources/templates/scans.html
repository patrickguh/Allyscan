Allyscan crawls your site, then scans each page

A worker takes the job from a Postgres queue, follows same-site links breadth-first and opens every page it finds in headless Chromium to run axe-core.

What each accessibility report holds

Files each issue under its WCAG number, like 1.4.3

Each finding carries the success criterion it fails, from 1.1.1 to 4.1.2, with its name and level.

Checks the standard Ontario law sets

Section 14 of O. Reg. 191/11, under the AODA, requires public sector organizations and businesses with 50 or more employees in Ontario to meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA on their websites.

“Designated public sector organizations and large organizations shall make their internet websites and web content conform with the World Wide Web Consortium Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0, initially at Level A and increasing to Level AA.”

Do I need an account to scan a website?

No. A scan without an account shows its report once and is deleted an hour later. An account keeps every report, private to you.

How many pages of a website does a scan check?

Up to 20 pages on the same site, found by following links from the address you give.

Does a clean accessibility report mean my site meets the AODA?

No. Automated checks find part of WCAG. Whether alt text is accurate or a form makes sense with a screen reader still takes a person.

Where do the fixes for a website’s issues come from?

Each rule links to its page on Deque University, written by the makers of axe-core.

What does it cost to scan a website?

Nothing. Scans are free, with or without an account.
